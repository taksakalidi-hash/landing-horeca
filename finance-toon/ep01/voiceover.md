# Broke by Friday, episode 1: voiceover text for ElevenLabs

Three voices, three separate files. Paste each block into ElevenLabs as is, including the `<break time="1.5s" />` tags: they put a pause between lines, and I use those pauses to cut the audio and line it up with the animation.

Save as: `narrator.mp3`, `dave.mp3`, `penny.mp3`.

Voices: NARRATOR is a calm, slightly ironic male narrator. DAVE is a tired 29-year-old guy, panicky and funny. PENNY is a sarcastic, confident female voice (a piggy bank).


## NARRATOR (36 lines)
```
Most people work forty years, earn over a million dollars, and still end up with almost nothing.
<break time="1.5s" />
Not because they earn too little. Because nobody ever taught them seven simple rules.
<break time="1.5s" />
In the next seven minutes, you'll learn all seven, and for each one, exactly what to do today. Rule number four alone can save you thousands of dollars.
<break time="1.5s" />
Rule one. Find out where your money really goes.
<break time="1.5s" />
You can't fix a leak you can't see. Most people guess their spending, and miss by hundreds of dollars a month.
<break time="1.5s" />
And it's usually not the coffee. It's the big three: housing, your car, and food, especially delivery.
<break time="1.5s" />
Here's what to do today. Open your banking app and look at last month. Sort every expense into three groups: needs, wants, and why did I buy that.
<break time="1.5s" />
Ten minutes and a notes app are enough. Almost everyone who does this finds money they didn't know they were losing.
<break time="1.5s" />
Rule two. Pay yourself first, automatically.
<break time="1.5s" />
Most people save whatever is left at the end of the month. And at the end of the month, nothing is left. So flip it.
<break time="1.5s" />
Set up an automatic transfer that moves money to savings on payday, before you can spend it.
<break time="1.5s" />
Start with ten percent. On a three thousand dollar paycheck, that's three hundred a month. Three thousand six hundred a year, without thinking about it.
<break time="1.5s" />
Action for today: set up one automatic transfer for your next payday. Even fifty dollars counts. The habit matters more than the amount.
<break time="1.5s" />
Rule three. Build an emergency fund before anything else.
<break time="1.5s" />
Life will throw rocks at you. A broken car. The dentist. A lost job. Without a cushion, every surprise goes on a credit card.
<break time="1.5s" />
The goal is three to six months of basic expenses. If you spend twenty-five hundred a month, that's seventy-five hundred to fifteen thousand.
<break time="1.5s" />
Sounds huge? Start with a first milestone of one thousand dollars. Keep it in a separate savings account. Safe, easy to reach, but not on your everyday card.
<break time="1.5s" />
Rule four. Kill high-interest debt. This is the one that saves thousands.
<break time="1.5s" />
A typical credit card charges over twenty percent interest a year.
<break time="1.5s" />
Say you owe three thousand dollars at twenty-two percent, and pay a hundred a month. It takes about forty-four months, and you hand the bank around fourteen hundred dollars in interest.
<break time="1.5s" />
Pay two hundred instead, and you're done in about eighteen months, with roughly five hundred forty in interest. Same debt. Almost nine hundred dollars saved.
<break time="1.5s" />
The method: pay the minimum on everything, then throw every extra dollar at the debt with the highest interest rate first.
<break time="1.5s" />
Rule five. Convert prices into hours of your life.
<break time="1.5s" />
Before you buy anything you don't need, divide the price by what you take home per hour. At twenty dollars an hour, those two hundred dollar sneakers cost ten hours of work.
<break time="1.5s" />
Still want them? Buy them guilt-free. Not sure? Wait thirty days. Most of the time, you won't even remember them.
<break time="1.5s" />
And while you're at it, check your statement for subscriptions. Many people pay for services they forgot they have.
<break time="1.5s" />
Rule six. When you earn more, don't spend more.
<break time="1.5s" />
You get a raise, and somehow you're still broke a year later. Economists call it lifestyle creep. Your income goes up, and your spending quietly follows.
<break time="1.5s" />
The fix: every time your income rises, save at least half of the increase. A three hundred dollar raise? One fifty for you, one fifty for your future.
<break time="1.5s" />
Rule seven. Make your money work, because cash slowly melts.
<break time="1.5s" />
With three percent inflation, ten thousand dollars under your mattress buys only about seventy-four hundred dollars' worth of stuff after ten years.
<break time="1.5s" />
So once you have an emergency fund and no expensive debt, invest for the long term. Two hundred dollars a month, growing around seven percent a year on average, becomes roughly two hundred forty thousand after thirty years.
<break time="1.5s" />
You only put in seventy-two thousand. The rest is compound interest. Nothing is guaranteed, markets go up and down, but time is your most powerful tool.
<break time="1.5s" />
So here's your checklist. Track where your money goes. Pay yourself first. Build an emergency fund. Kill expensive debt. Convert prices into hours. Save half of every raise. And let your money grow.
<break time="1.5s" />
Don't do all seven this week. Pick one, and do it today. A free money template with all seven rules is in the pinned comment.
<break time="1.5s" />
Next time on Broke by Friday: how scammers steal money from smart people, and the one question that stops them.
```
