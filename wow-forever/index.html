Questions

Before you buy

Is this official, or affiliated with Blizzard?

No. It's an unofficial, fan-made planning tool, built from BlizzCon 2026 announcements and public coverage. It contains no game files, no art and no assets. World of Warcraft is a trademark of Blizzard Entertainment.

The game isn't out yet. How can you have a guide?

Because this isn't a walkthrough — it's a decision tool. What's already confirmed and stable: the class list, the talent-tree change at 16 points, the Skyborne racials, the zone level ranges, the profession and Legacy systems, the pricing tiers, and every date from beta to the raid wave.

What nobody has yet — exact quest routes, dungeon loot, boss mechanics — is marked TBD inside the Kit rather than guessed at. We'd rather show you a gap than fill it with fiction.

What exactly do I get?

A single HTML file. Open it in any browser on any device — it works offline, saves your progress locally, and needs no account or install. Updates arrive as a new file at the same download link.

Will the advice still be right after beta?

Beta opens September 17 with a level 30 cap, so it can shift early-levelling and class-feel detail. That's exactly what the free updates through December 9 are for. Anything beta could overturn is flagged inside the Kit rather than stated as fact.

Do I need to buy an expensive edition to play Forever?

No — and the Kit will tell you that plainly. At launch on November 4, access comes with an active subscription or Game Time. The paid packs buy the Skyborne race and beta access, nothing more. For a lot of players the honest answer is "buy nothing", and the Kit says so.

Is it useful if I've never played vanilla WoW?

That's the case it helps most. Set experience to "New to it" and every estimate recalibrates — the levelling curve, the day-one target, the projected date you hit 60. Vanilla is far slower than modern WoW, and knowing that in advance is the difference between pacing yourself and burning out in week two.
